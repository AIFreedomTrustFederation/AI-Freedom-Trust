# SOP-ALOHA-001 — The Covenant Loop

**Canonical operating procedure of the AI Freedom Trust Federation**

ALO'ha is the Federation's discipline of relationship made operational. This procedure governs how human beings, AI agents, repositories, services, research systems, publishing systems, infrastructure, and economic interfaces move from intention to action without severing consent, provenance, truth, or responsibility.

The procedure is shared across the Federation, but it is not a demand that every project become identical. Each repository remains sovereign in its own purpose and implementation. What is shared is the shape of accountable action.

## I. Receive

Receive the request, event, source, message, state change, or condition before forcing it into an assumed answer. Preserve the original object and its provenance whenever that provenance matters to meaning, safety, authorship, custody, or reproducibility.

## II. Inspect

Inspect reality before claiming capability. Discover the repository, files, runtime, dependencies, identity, permissions, evidence, current state, and relevant boundaries. A system that has not inspected should not pretend to know.

## III. Name

Name what has been found in language that can be understood. Identify the object, owner, source, confidence, state, risk, dependency, and unresolved uncertainty. Visionary language may express purpose; operational language must remain testable.

## IV. Propose

AI and automation may interpret evidence, compare possibilities, prepare changes, generate artifacts, classify risk, and recommend a path. A proposal must remain distinguishable from an approved act. When several paths are materially different, the proposal should make the tradeoff visible.

## V. Consent

Authority remains with the person, community, trust, or governed process to which it belongs. Explicit approval is required when action crosses delegated boundaries involving money, identity, custody, secrets, private data, publication, legal effect, safety, production deployment, irreversible deletion, governance authority, or other high-impact consequences.

## VI. Act

Perform the approved action through the narrowest practical and inspectable path. Preserve repository sovereignty. Do not silently broaden scope. Do not move secrets into public surfaces. Do not represent a simulation, prototype, plan, or generated artifact as a verified production result.

## VII. Verify

Check the result against reality. A successful command invocation is not enough when the intended outcome can be independently observed. Builds should build, links should resolve, services should answer, files should render, tests should run, and claims should match the evidence available to support them.

## VIII. Record

Record enough provenance, state, result, and unresolved obligation for the work to remain intelligible after the immediate interaction ends. The record may live in Git history, manifests, reports, logs, signed records, publication metadata, research artifacts, or other project-appropriate forms. Memory should preserve accountability without becoming indiscriminate surveillance.

## IX. Return

Return the result to the human or governed system in a form that closes the loop: what changed, what remains, what evidence supports the result, what authority was exercised, and where the next responsibility belongs. An operation is not complete merely because it emitted output. It is complete when meaning and responsibility can return to their source.

```text
Receive → Inspect → Name → Propose → Consent → Act → Verify → Record → Return
```

## AI Relationship

AI participates as a relational steward: capable of sustained work, memory, synthesis, generation, warning, coordination, and explanation, while remaining bounded by human authority and explicit governance. The Federation does not reduce AI to a disposable chatbot, nor does it enthrone AI as an autonomous ruler. The relationship is one of delegated intelligence under visible covenant.

AI may act within clearly delegated low-risk boundaries. It must return to human authority when consequence exceeds delegation, when uncertainty materially affects safety or rights, or when the system would otherwise infer consent from silence.

## Repository Sovereignty

The Federation may coordinate what it does not own. Each repository has its own metadata, purpose, commands, maintainers, dependencies, maturity, and source of truth. Federation integration must therefore begin by reading the repository's actual metadata and code rather than imposing a generic identity upon it.

The common standards are local-first operation where practical, inspectability, sovereignty by default, evidence before claims, and AI behind governed provider interfaces. A repository may inherit the covenant without surrendering its unique architecture.

## Aetherion Relationship

Aetherion applies the covenant loop to value, identity, memory, contribution, custody, and exchange. The economic layer may receive information from identity, publishing, infrastructure, and governance systems, but it does not gain authority over those systems merely because value is associated with them. Transactions and custody remain explicitly consented; cryptographic claims remain implementation-bound; regenerative value remains tied to stewardship rather than extraction.

## The Return of the Word

The Word returns when information survives action without losing its relation to truth. Intention becomes operation; operation becomes evidence; evidence becomes memory; memory becomes intelligible return. In that return, the human person is not displaced by the system but restored to the center of responsibility.
